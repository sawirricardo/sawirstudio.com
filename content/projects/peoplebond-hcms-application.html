PeopleBond HCMS Application

An HR and payroll platform, its employee app and its website, built from inside the team since 2019

The client

PeopleBond is a human capital management system (HCMS) for Indonesian companies, covering the employee lifecycle from hiring to payroll. It runs in the cloud or on a company’s own servers.

The outcome

  • Companies run their payroll on it. PeopleBond’s customers include Rekayasa Engineering, Eatwell Culinary (Ichiban Sushi, Ta Wan, Dapur Solo), Mercure Hotels, PNM and Bhinneka.
  • Payroll kept up with the law. When PP 58/2023 brought the TER rates and tax filing moved to Coretax, PeopleBond’s payroll followed.
  • HR moved to the phone. Employees tap in, ask for leave and download payslips in pbConnect, on the App Store and Google Play, instead of filling in a form and chasing a signature.
  • Seven years, no rewrite. The same crew has carried the product since 2019, so each change builds on the last instead of starting over.

How we work together

We’ve worked with PeopleBond since 2019, as part of their crew rather than as an agency on the outside. We build alongside their own developer and pick up whatever the product needs next: a payroll rule, an App Store review, a new website.

The HR platform

The web application covers employees, recruitment, timesheets, payroll, leave, competency, evaluation and training, with a documentation site for the companies that use it.

Payroll is where Indonesian HR software earns its keep, because the tax rules keep moving. PeopleBond calculates PPh 21 with the TER rates from PP 58/2023, A, B or C by PTKP status, and recalculates the year at progressive rates in December. Tax forms come out ready for Coretax and e-Bupot, BPJS reports are built in, and leave requests can reach approvers on WhatsApp, Telegram or LINE.

The employee app

pbConnect puts the everyday HR tasks in each employee’s pocket. It is on the App Store and Google Play.

  • Tap in and out. Attendance with geotagging, a selfie checked by on-device face detection where the company turns it on, or a QR code at the office.
  • Requests and approvals. Leave, absence, overtime, shift changes, business trips and attendance claims, with approvals for managers and a view of their team.
  • Pay and company info. Payslips, the working calendar, the organisation structure, company policies and news.
  • Notifications. Push notifications and an inbox, so a request never waits unseen.
  • Two languages. English and Indonesian.

How it grew

  • 2021. Work starts in November: sign-in, tap in by QR code, the working calendar and a manager’s view of their team.
  • 2022. Leave, absence and overtime requests with approvals, push notifications, a photo at tap in, and timesheets. The app passes Apple’s review and is on both stores.
  • 2023. Payslip downloads, a tap history showing where each tap happened, English and Indonesian, business trip approvals, and QR codes that expire.
  • 2024. Shift change requests and on-device face detection for the tap in selfie, then a full upgrade of the app underneath, so it keeps up with new phones.
  • 2026. Break times with reminders, a rebuilt team view with filters, a new home screen, and scripted releases that build and sign both apps.

The website

In 2026 we rebuilt peoplebond.id. It’s in English and Indonesian, follows the visitor’s light or dark setting, and has a page for each module and integration. A demo request is saved for the sales team before it opens a WhatsApp chat, so a lead isn’t lost when the chat never starts. Before the payroll and tax pages went up, we checked what they say against the HCMS code, so the site doesn’t promise anything the product doesn’t do.

How we approached it

Attendance is where an HR app gets tested every single day, and where people are most tempted to cheat. So tap in checks where the employee is, can ask for a face in the selfie, and the app won’t run on a jailbroken or rooted phone. Everything else follows the same idea: an employee should be able to do in a minute on their phone what used to take a form and a signature.

And because we’ve been on the product for years, a change doesn’t start with relearning the system. Platform upgrades, new Apple requirements and new tax rules get handled as they come, not saved up for a rewrite.
