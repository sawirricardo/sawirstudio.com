# What Is a Forward Deployed Engineer, and When Should You Borrow One?

A forward deployed engineer is a software engineer who works inside a customer's team, not back at the office. They sit where the problem is, learn how the customer's work really runs, and ship software into it. In 2026 it became one of the most talked-about jobs in tech.

## Where the title comes from

Palantir made it famous. It was sending “forward deployed” engineers, a phrase borrowed from the military, to work on site with its customers [by 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_Deployed_Engineer). The idea was simple: the hard part of enterprise software isn't the product, it's fitting it to one organization's messy data, systems and habits. So you send engineers who can write code on the spot.

## Why it's everywhere now

AI made the gap between buying software and getting value from it very visible. A company can license a model in an afternoon, but wiring it into its own data, permissions and daily work takes engineering. So the companies selling AI started hiring people to do exactly that:

- **OpenAI** launched the OpenAI Deployment Company on May 11, 2026, organized around engineers embedded with clients ([KDnuggets](https://www.kdnuggets.com/forward-deployed-engineer-ais-hottest-new-career-or-consulting-with-a-better-title)).
- **AWS** put $1 billion into a new unit to embed engineers with customers, and **Microsoft** committed $2.5 billion and 6,000 people to an AI implementation unit, both reported by CNBC in mid-2026 ([Wikipedia's summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_Deployed_Engineer)).
- Job postings followed. Indeed and the Financial Times found monthly listings for the role rose more than 800% between January and September 2025 ([KDnuggets](https://www.kdnuggets.com/forward-deployed-engineer-ais-hottest-new-career-or-consulting-with-a-better-title)).

## How it differs from a consultant

A consultant tells you what to change. A forward deployed engineer changes it: they have access to your systems and they ship code. Strictly, the title belongs to engineers at product companies, and what they learn at the customer shapes what their company builds next. Without that loop, some argue, it's consulting with a better title.

For a company buying help, though, the label matters less than the shape of the work: an engineer who sits inside your team for a while, learns your systems, and leaves working software behind.

## Should you hire one?

Probably not, if you're a small or mid-sized company. The big AI companies are competing for these people, and senior roles are posted at prices most businesses can't match. Many companies only need one for months: long enough to get AI and their systems working together, not forever.

That's where borrowing one makes sense. Bring in an engineer who works inside your team, in your chat and your repositories, for as long as the job takes. It's worth it when:

- you've bought AI tools and nothing changed in how the work gets done,
- the problem spans several systems, like an ERP, a store, payments and a pile of spreadsheets,
- and nobody in-house has the time to own it.

It isn't when you already know exactly what to build. Then a project specified up front is the quicker way. And if you'll need the role for good, hire for it.
