Services Is Next: What a Few People with AI Agents Can Deliver

A short thread went around this week. Ethan Mollick said he keeps hearing from big firms, from manufacturers to financial services, that teams of two or three people using AI now do the work of dozens. BuccoCapital answered that firms either adopt the new technology or get run over by startups that did, and that after software, services is next. Aaron Levie called it a huge opportunity to build services firms with agents at the center: the expertise matters as much as ever, but delivery gets a 10X multiplier.

We already work this way.

What it looks like from the inside

A few people, with AI agents doing most of the typing, deliver what used to take a much bigger team. Some recent examples:

  • A full website in under a week. We rebuilt PeopleBond's website between November 23 and 26, 2026: Indonesian and English, a page for each module, integration and industry, and free payroll calculators. Before the payroll and tax pages went up, we checked them against the product's code.
  • An app in three months. DBTV went from its first build in late June 2025 to the App Store in September, and has shipped more than twenty updates since.
  • Our own work. Seven apps of our own on the App Store, and this site in twelve languages.

Expertise still decides the outcome

Levie is right that the expertise matters as much as ever. Agents write code quickly; they don't know what to build. The value is in knowing that Indonesian payroll has to follow the TER rates from PP 58/2023 and recalculate the year in December, that a kilo of chilies at a market stall isn't a neatly packed product, or that Shopify payouts don't match your books for reasons an accountant needs explained. AI does the typing and we do the thinking: a person reads, tests and answers for every line that ships.

What it means for clients

  • Smaller businesses can afford real systems. Clinics, cooperatives and shops that were never worth a big agency's time can now get one system in place of the spreadsheets, at a price that makes sense for them.
  • Pay for the outcome, not the hours. When delivery is this fast, billing by the hour rewards the wrong thing. We'd rather agree on what changes and one fixed price before we start.
  • An outside engineer can cut through the politics. Mollick's point was that small AI teams inside big firms are causing fights over what it all means. Someone from outside, working inside your team for as long as the job takes, can get it done without being part of the fight. That's our forward deployed engineering.

What doesn't change

What clients buy is a result, and someone accountable for it. Speed only helps if the thing works next year too, so we stay to look after what we build: PeopleBond has worked with us since 2019.

If something is slowing your business down, tell us what it is.
